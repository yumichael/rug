# Direct sampling of monomial pattern classes

A normalized monomial on a complete odd residue class has two coordinates. The first is an odd slope and the second is a geometric ratio. For pre-twists, a falling-factorial basis gives independent binary coordinates. Together these constructions give exact uniform sampling by integer rank, with no field generation, class catalogue, or rejection step during sampling.

This extends the [first-pass modulus results](report.md) and the [analysis of twists and selectors](modulus-and-twists.md). The [no-pre XOR theory](../2026-10-13-post-power/2026-10-13-xor-post-power-theory.md) already classifies monomials on the complete odd input set. Here the geometric ratio gives a direct proof on a general principal-unit coset, and the pre-map analysis resolves overlaps across spatial fields. The previous catalogue provides an independent check at small sides.

## Coverage

Use a displayed side $N=2^r\ge16$, an even pre-power $q=2^w u$ with $u$ odd, a positive post-power $p=2^v j$ with $j$ odd, and an odd post-multiplier. A twist is $f(z)=z+2z(z-1)Q(z)$, with integer polynomial $Q$. Checkerboard-opposite uses original coordinates $(x+y,y-x-1)$ and pre-modulus $K=N2^w$. Odd-odd uses $(2x+1,2y+1)$ and $K=2N2^w$. Classes are anchored at the first displayed cell. They retain spatial positions, and a numerical shift class quotients the common circular color offset.

| Sampling and family                              | Scope of the direct union at Minimum period                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Checkerboard-opposite, fixed pre-power and twist | All post-powers and odd multipliers for all four combiners                                                              |
| Checkerboard-opposite, top-bit head              | All four combiners as separate families, across every pre-power in the group and every twist in the linear-twist regime |
| Checkerboard-opposite, erased tail               | Every pre-power and post-power at identity twist, or every pre-power and twist with odd post-power                      |
| Odd-odd AND and OR                               | All pre-powers, post-powers, and twists, including the union of the two combiners                                       |
| Odd-odd MULT                                     | All pre-powers and post-powers at identity twist, or all twists and post-powers at a fixed pre-valuation                |
| Odd-odd ADD and CLMUL                            | All pre-powers with $w\ge r-3$, all post-powers and twists                                                              |
| Odd-odd, fixed pre-power and twist               | All post-powers for AND, OR, ADD, MULT and CLMUL. All odd post-powers for XOR and ABSDIFF                               |

The table concerns uniform circular order and equality sampling. Order and numerical shift classes agree within these spaces. The two distinction-preserving selectors also admit a direct **shift** sampler conditional on a fixed pre-power and twist for the four checkerboard combiners and the five odd-odd unit cases. When all three selectors agree, the Minimum period order results apply directly. Incomplete value grids at larger precision can introduce an order quotient.

## Two coordinates for a unit post-map

Let the reachable unit values be congruent to $1$ modulo $2^s$, with $s\ge2$ and unit modulus $U=2^sT$, where $T$ is dyadic. Use $g=1+2^s$ as generator and write a value as $g^b\pmod U$. Odd-odd ADD first factors two out of the raw value. Checkerboard head ABSDIFF with even post-power can use the signed difference. These operations give the relevant unit values with anchor one.

The post-modulus for the unit power is $U2^v$. Write $\lambda=g^p\bmod T$ and $\eta=(g^p-1)/2^{s+v}$, an odd integer by lifting. Subtract the value at the anchor and divide the residue by $2^{s+v}$. The resulting code is

$$
F(b)=a\eta\sum_{i=0}^{b-1}\lambda^i\pmod T.
$$

The odd first difference $A=a\eta\bmod T$ and the ratio $\lambda$ are independent. The odd multiplier ranges over all odd residues modulo $T$, and $p$ generates the cyclic subgroup of $g$ modulo $T$. The all-power space therefore has $(T/2)\max(1,T/2^s)$ classes, with $p=1,\ldots,\max(1,T/2^s)$ as representatives. For even powers, use the group generated by $g^2$ and multiply the representative powers by two. Its count is $(T/2)\max(1,T/2^{s+1})$.

On a complete coset, $F(1)=A$ and $F(2)=A(1+\lambda)$ identify the two parameters. The normalized values fill the complete $T$-point grid by the lifting criterion on differences. Hence an anchored order gives the numerical shift class as well. There is one equality class within a unit branch, since the post-map permutes the raw residues at its effective precision.

### Two probes in an incomplete coset

It is enough that the reachable indices include an odd $b_1$ and a $b_2$ congruent to two modulo four. Put $S_\lambda(b)=\sum_{i<b}\lambda^i$. The ratio $S_\lambda(b_2)/S_\lambda(b_1)$ recovers $\lambda$ on the odd ratios. Cross-multiply the difference of this ratio at $\lambda,\mu$. The numerator factors by $\lambda-\mu$, and the remaining factor is odd. Its parity is the cross-product derivative at one, namely $\binom{b_2}2 b_1-b_2\binom{b_1}2$, which is odd. Then $F(b_1)$ recovers $A$.

A complete low-precision projection of the raw coset supplies these probes at Minimum period and at the preservation precision for the stated unit cases. At checkerboard pre-power two with OR the twist can leave the coset incomplete. The even operand zero still exposes the full odd-operand coset $1\pmod8$, including the required $b_2$ in the $1\pmod4$ representation. OR of pre-value one with the even pre-value of original input two gives an odd $b_1$, because that even value has valuation two. At Minimum period the even code grid is complete and its cells are known from the zero even operand. Their order fixes all even positions, and each gap holds exactly one possible odd position. Thus order still recovers shift at this fixed base.

At preservation precision the probes recover shift classes even when the value grid has larger gaps. The unit modulus $U$ comes from the [selector formulas](modulus-and-twists.md). For low-valuation odd-odd MULT and CLMUL, computing it can require one raw-value calculation at setup.

### The signed comparison branch

For checkerboard head ABSDIFF, odd post-powers at Minimum period all produce one two-color comparison class at a fixed base. Even post-powers have more colors, so their order and equality supports are disjoint from the odd branch.

At the preservation modulus $K$, the odd branch has $(K/4)(T/2)$ shift classes, where $T=K/2^{\min(q,w+2)}$. Choose $p$ odd modulo $T$ and $a$ odd modulo $K/2$. Use a nonzero even pre-value $e$ divisible by $2^{w+2}$ as in the [precision proof](modulus-and-twists.md#checkerboard-opposite-selectors). The signed inputs $x=K-e+1$ and $x-K=1-e$ both occur. Their absolute values are negatives of each other modulo $K$. After odd powering their anchored sum recovers $-2a$. A principal-unit generator then determines $p\bmod T$. For $q\ge4$ it comes from the zero even operand. For $q=2$, pair the valuation-two even operand with odd pre-value one. Accounting for the sign, the signed value is $5\pmod8$ and generates the principal units modulo $K$. The two probe cells at $x$ and $x-K$ are antipodal for even powers at $2K2^v$, while for odd powers their difference modulo $K$ has valuation one. The shift branches are disjoint.

### Odd powers on the nonunit odd-odd tables

For odd-odd XOR remove the common $S^p$, where $S=2^{w+2}$. The resulting modulus is $T=N/2=2^t$, and the basic value $B$ ranges over $0,\ldots,T-1$. Odd powers permute the complete odd grid. Its positions are fixed by the order starting from the zero anchor, and each gap holds at most one even position. Thus order recovers the numerical shift.

For odd $p<t$, the image of $B=2$ has valuation exactly $p$ and distinguishes the row. The value at $B=1$ identifies the odd multiplier. For odd $p\ge t$ all even values vanish, and the odd-unit cycle gives $T/8$ possible powers. There are $(T/2)(\lfloor t/2\rfloor+T/8)$ orders. The number of groups among inputs of valuation one is $2^{t-p-1}$ for $p<t$ and their values are nonzero. In the tail those inputs merge into the zero group. This gives $\lfloor t/2\rfloor+1$ equality classes. For ABSDIFF the normalized modulus is four. The rows $p=1$ and odd $p\ge3$ give four and three colors respectively, with two orders in each row.

## Direct coordinates for pre-twists

On the odd branch put $s=w+2$, $S=2^s$, $z=1+SB$, and use effective code width $T=2^t$. The normalized twist functions are exactly

$$
B+\sum_{k\ge1}2S^{k-1}c_k(B)_k\pmod T,\qquad (B)_k=B(B-1)\cdots(B-k+1).
$$

The raw twist increment after normalization is $2B(1+SB)Q(1+SB)$. Its monomial weights $2S^{k-1}$ have free coefficients because multiplication by $1+SB$ is triangular with diagonal one in the Taylor variable $SB$. The change from monomials to falling factorials preserves the weights, by triangular integer changes of basis. The forward difference of order $k$ at zero of $(B)_k$ is $k!$ and the other difference coordinates are zero. Newton expansion therefore gives one independent digit $c_k$ modulo $2^{\max(0,t-1-d_k)}$, where

$$
d_k=s(k-1)+\nu_2(k!).
$$

Call the resulting group of twist actions $G_s$. Each action is a permutation since its linear coefficient is odd and all higher coefficients are even. The weights are closed under composition and the inverse is a positive iterate. To emit native $Q$, divide the increment by $2B$, use $S^{k-1}(B)_k/B=\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}(z-1-Si)$, then divide by $z$ using its truncated inverse in $z-1$.

### How much the odd part of the pre-power adds

Use the original odd operand index $b$ given by a power of 5, with a possible sign. The even pre-power removes the sign. The base code at valuation $w$ is

$$
L_w(b)=\frac{5^{2^w b}-1}{2^s}\pmod T.
$$

An odd part $u$ acts by $P_u(B)=((1+SB)^u-1)/S$. Different odd parts contribute the right cosets $G_sP_u$, and two cosets agree exactly when $P_{u/u'}$ belongs to $G_s$.

Let $D$ be the largest $k$ with $d_k<t$. In falling factorials with weights $S^{k-1}$, the parity of the coefficient of degree $k$ of $P_u$ is $\binom uk\bmod2$. All higher monomials contribute an extra factor $S$, and the Newton coordinate detects this parity for $k\le D$. The condition for $P_u$ to belong to $G_s$ is therefore that $\binom uk$ is even for $2\le k\le D$. Over the binary field, expanding $(1+y)^u$ as the product of $1+y^{2^i}$ over the set bits of $u$ shows that this is equivalent to $u\equiv1\pmod{2^{1+\lfloor\log_2D\rfloor}}$. Thus the number of pre-maps at one valuation is

$$
B_s=2^{\lfloor\log_2D\rfloor+\sum_{k\ge1}\max(0,t-1-d_k)}.
$$

A rank selects an odd part modulo $2^{1+\lfloor\log_2D\rfloor}$ and then the independent twist digits. Without twists the pre-map count is $T/2$, because the value at original exponent index one determines $u$.

Distinct rows with $s<t$ cannot overlap. In the original index $b$, the second difference at zero of $P_uL_w$ has valuation exactly $s$. The twist's odd linear coefficient preserves it, and all higher terms contribute differences divisible by $2^{s+1}$. For $s\ge t$, all rows are the same odd linear maps on $b$.

Odd powers normalize $G_s$. In the formal variable $y=SB$, the twist is the identity modulo two and a power acts by $(1+y)^u-1$. Conjugate by that power and a power whose exponent is its inverse modulo $T$. Their exponents multiply to one modulo $T$, so $(1+y)^T=1+y^T$ over the binary field makes the conjugate the identity in every degree below $T$. Higher degrees vanish at the required precision after substituting $y=SB$. This proves the normalization property for the actual finite actions.

## Full odd-odd AND and OR unions

Here $T=N/2$. The normalized raw table is the Boolean meet or join of the two operand codes. Suppose two fields have the same equality partition. The diagonal identifies a relabeling of their operand codes, and the full Cartesian table forces it to preserve meet or join. It is a Boolean lattice automorphism, hence determined by a permutation of the bit positions. The two pre-maps are also 2-adic isometries. Each singleton bit's distance from zero identifies its bit position, so the relabeling fixes every singleton and is the identity. Different pre-maps give disjoint order spaces.

Each pre-map at $w$ has $(T/2)\max(1,T/2^{w+2})$ orders. Sum the products of the pre-map and post counts over $w=1,\ldots,t-2$, where the last row represents the common linear tail. Equality sampling weights each row by its pre-map count alone. AND and OR have disjoint supports because the axis with other operand code zero is constant for AND and varies for OR.

At side 64 the twisted AND/OR union has 5,632 orders and 128 equalities. At side 4096 it has 589,842,874,368 orders. The sampler stores one row per productive pre-valuation and a few integer coefficient widths per row.

## Pre and post composition

In the erased checkerboard tail the four combiners return the odd pre-operand. At identity twist, powering the reduced $x^q$ agrees with powering $x$ by $qp$ at the chosen post-modulus, by lifting. Odd-odd MULT at Minimum period has the same reduction on $xy$ because $x^q y^q=(xy)^q\pmod K$. In both cases the allowed effective exponents $qp$ are exactly even up to the relevant exponent cycle, and the geometric ratios are generated by $5^2$ modulo $T$. Here $T=N/4$ for the checkerboard and $T=N/2$ for odd-odd. A fixed pre-power of valuation one reaches every ratio as the post-power varies. For the checkerboard choose that representative far enough out to erase the even branch. The untwisted union has $(T/2)\max(1,T/8)$ orders and one equality in either sampling.

In the erased checkerboard with twists and odd post-powers, the pre-map index alone covers the union. The odd post-power normalizes $G_s$ and can be absorbed into the odd part of the pre-power. The output multiplier is a linear member of $G_s$. Thus post-power and multiplier one suffice, and the count is the sum of $B_s$ over the disjoint pre-rows.

For odd-odd MULT with twists at fixed $w$, use the logarithm of the unit. The raw table modulo $K$ is addition of the two operand logs. The zero axis and full Cartesian table force any relabeling between equal partitions to be an additive automorphism of those logs. This is multiplication by an odd residue, or left composition by an odd power in unit coordinates. Its action is free and preserves the pre-map space by normalization. Each orbit has the same conditional order support because a common odd power on the operands is absorbed into the post-power. The counts are $B_s/(T/2)$ for equalities and $(B_s/(T/2))(T/2)\max(1,T/2^s)$ for orders. The sampler fixes the code at original operand five to its value at $u=1$. For every odd-part coset and higher Newton digits, the first Newton digit reaches that target exactly once.

## The top-bit checkerboard head

The powers in this group satisfy $q=r+w-1$. The even operand is zero or $K/2$, according to whether its original input is divisible by four. This includes both 6 and 8 at side 64. Use $T=N/4=2^t$ and $H=T/2$.

For XOR, ADD and even-post ABSDIFF, the even branch either leaves the odd operand code alone or adds $H$. Every unit post-map commutes with that toggle. The zero even operand exposes the complete odd-branch order, which determines both slices. At identity twist the geometric ratios across pre-valuations form nested groups. The smallest $w$ suffices, with the generator squared for the even-post branch. Each of these spaces has one equality partition.

If $s=w+2\ge t-2$ at the smallest pre-valuation, the twist coordinates above the linear term have width zero, so twists on the odd branch are odd scalings. The normalized unit post is, up to an odd multiplier,

$$
B+2^{s-1}(p-1)B^2\pmod T.
$$

The higher monomials vanish because the coefficient at degree $k\ge3$ has valuation at least $s(k-1)-\nu_2(k)$. An odd scaling $cB$ can be absorbed by choosing $p'$ with $p'-1\equiv c(p-1)$ at the precision of the quadratic term and adjusting the output multiplier. The parity of $p$ can be preserved. The untwisted representatives therefore cover the full twisted XOR, ADD and even-post ABSDIFF spaces in this regime.

For OR, the equality relation between the two even-branch slices records the top bit of each odd pre-code. For odd-post ABSDIFF the binary comparison records the same bit. Different odd scalings give different top-bit tables at fixed $w$. An odd multiplication preserves the lower half of a dyadic ring only when it is the identity. Use input one if the multiplier lies in the upper half, or the first positive input reaching the upper half if the multiplier is at least three and below that half.

The top-bit tables also distinguish different active pre-valuations in the linear-twist regime. The map from a smaller $s$ row to a larger one is $B\mapsto aB+bB^2$ with $a$ odd and $\nu_2(b)=s-1$, from the even composition formula. For $s=t-1$, preservation on even inputs forces $a\equiv1\pmod H$ by the lower-half lemma. On odd inputs the map then adds a nonzero constant on each residue class modulo four. Such a translation cannot preserve the lower-half interval of that progression. For $s=t-2$, even inputs instead force $a+2b\equiv1\pmod H$. On odd inputs the shift is $b(1-2B)$ plus a multiple of $H$. It is again nonzero and constant on each progression modulo four. For $s\ge t$, the maps are linear and matching slopes share their rows. Thus the OR and odd-post ABSDIFF rows are indexed by their pre-slopes and post coordinates. The odd and even ABSDIFF branches are disjoint since their color counts differ.

At side 64 the twisted group $\{6,8\}$ has 192 OR orders, 16 each for XOR and ADD, and 24 for ABSDIFF, counted as separate combiner families. The equality counts are 16, 1, 1 and 17 respectively.

## High pre-valuations in odd-odd ADD and CLMUL

Here $T=N/2$ and $w\ge r-3$, so the pre-code is an odd scalar times the original exponent index, and all twists are linear. In ADD the sum code is an odd multiple of the sum of two original indices. At the first pre-row $s=t$, the post uses unit exponent $s-1$. It gives the linear shape and $B+2^{t-2}B^2$, both with arbitrary odd output scaling. Input scaling is absorbed using the quadratic formula. Higher pre-rows give only the linear shape. This gives one equality and $T$ orders.

For CLMUL, the product term vanishes at the minimum precision, leaving XOR of the scaled indices. Two pre-slopes have equal partitions exactly when their ratio acts as a binary linear map, since the relabeling must preserve XOR on the whole Cartesian table. The only binary-linear odd integer multiplications modulo $T$ are $1$ and $1+T/2$. If the multiplier has an additional set bit below the top bit, test $1,2^i$ at its lowest such bit. Their images overlap in bit $i$, so their ordinary sum differs from their XOR. The top-bit multiplier is binary-linear and can be absorbed by an odd post-multiplier. Thus there are $T/4$ equalities and $(T/4)(T/2)$ orders, with one pre-slope per odd residue below $T/2$.

## Implementation and verification

[direct_sampler.py](direct_sampler.py) decodes a bounded uniform integer into native pre- and post-operations. Each sampler has a separate equality index. For example, an order draw from the full twisted odd-odd AND/OR space can be made as follows, with the research folder on the Python module path.

```python
from random import Random

from direct_sampler import OddBitwiseSampler

sampler = OddBitwiseSampler(4096, twists=True)
spec = sampler.sample_spec(Random(0))
```

The spec receives an independent uniform color offset on the witness's modular grid. The sampler allocates no pattern signatures. The distinction-preserving low-valuation product cases compute raw precision once at setup.

[verify_direct.py](verify_direct.py) checks exact class sets against the previous finite method, verifies twist coordinates independently, and checks native workbench fields. The local test of 10,000 side-4096 AND/OR draws took about 0.03 seconds after setup, excluding rendering. [Recorded results](direct-results.json) contain the counts and check totals.

Run from the repository root.

```sh
MPLCONFIGDIR=/private/tmp/rug-research-mpl PYTHONPATH=research/interactive PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 research/interactive/.venv/bin/python research/theory/2026-11-19-monomial-first-pass/verify_direct.py
```

Outstanding unions include checkerboard heads with multiple surviving even values, the erased checkerboard tail with arbitrary twists and even post-powers together, and odd-odd MULT with arbitrary twists across pre-valuations. Odd-odd XOR and ABSDIFF with even post-powers still use the earlier finite order catalogue. At preservation precision, order equivalence can identify different numerical shifts. For example, checkerboard head ABSDIFF at side 16, pre-power four, identity twist and post-power one has raw labels 1, 15, 17, 33 and 49. Its preservation modulus is 64. Multipliers one and five give anchored numerators $(0,7,8,16,24)$ and $(0,3,8,16,24)$ over 32, in the same order. In this case the 36 shifts give 10 orders with multiplicities one, four, and five, so sampling the shifts uniformly gives different probabilities to those orders. The direct shift sampler leaves this quotient open.
